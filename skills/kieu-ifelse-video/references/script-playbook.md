# Script playbook

Measured targets for Vietnamese. For another language, keep the structure and aim for 6-7 minutes of narration.

## Title
- Starts with "Nếu", ends with "?", 6-10 syllables, Title Case, no emoji, no all caps, no shock words.
- Changes one ability of the viewer. The subject is "Bạn", "Của Bạn" or a shared experience such as "Một Giây".
- A concrete extreme makes the premise clear in under two seconds: gấp 10, gấp 100, 500, bằng không, mãi mãi. Either direction works.
- The topic stays in the viewer's body or mind: memory, attention, time sense, reflexes, senses, pain, sleep, strength, metabolism, emotion, ageing.
- Accept a premise only if real science predicts several layers of consequences, including a hidden cost.
- Do not repeat or rephrase an earlier title. A stronger step on a proven premise is allowed about once in five.

## Format
- 1,300-1,500 syllables (hard limits 1,150 and 1,650; EP02 ran 1,616 and came to 7:39).
- One paragraph of pure speech. No headings, stage directions, speaker names, quotation marks, timestamps, emoji or markdown.
- Dialogue sits in the flow as its own short sentence. Make the speaker obvious from context or a name in the line.
- Numbers as words, except years. Metric units.

## Voice
- Second person, present tense: the viewer is the protagonist, who may also have a name others use. "Bạn" about 4-5 times per 100 syllables.
- Very short sentences: average 5-8 syllables, about half at six or fewer, almost none over twenty. Fragments are welcome.
- 18-25 "..." placed just before the point lands. 15-20 questions, mixing the narrator's and the characters'.
- Contrast punches: two opposed fragments side by side.
- Fact, then "Bạn?": a real human baseline followed by the protagonist's extreme.
- Occasional dry humour to break tension. Warm and family-friendly: real danger, no gore, no swearing, no cruelty as comedy.

## Beats (syllable budgets for a 1,400-syllable script)
1. **Cold open, 80-140.** Small everyday miracles ending on a funny detail, or jump to the crisis and rewind.
2. **Baseline, rule, question, 80-140.** A real average, the extreme, then **one rule that limits the power**. That rule decides the climax. Say the title question out loud.
3. **Consequences, 350-500.** Six to ten quick beats, each with a situation, a real number and a result. Escalate from daily life to impressive to world scale. Mix upsides and downsides, and label them ("Điểm cộng", "Điểm trừ").
4. **Hidden cost, 110-210.** Something nobody mentions, tied to a real mechanism.
5. **The heart, 110-210.** The most important relationship. Plant one small specific detail to pay off later.
6. **Crisis and choice, 210-350.** An emergency the power almost solves but the rule blocks. Slow time with exact numbers. Reduce it to a two-way choice in one line.
7. **Ending, 140-210.** The protagonist chooses people over glory, money or the power. Never violence or revenge. Pay off the planted detail. Show the crowd's reaction, then a quiet line.
8. **"Có thật không?", 80-170, about half of episodes.** Step out of the story, separate fact from fiction, name real researchers only where they are real, end on an open question.
9. **Hook to the next episode, 30-70.** One mysterious door.
10. **Call to action, 35-65.** One specific question for the comments, then like and subscribe for Phần Hai, or for more Nếu Như when an arc ends.

## Cast and world
- Protagonist plus two to four others, short common names, a familiar Vietnamese setting.
- Side characters speak briefly, each with a clear role. Give the protagonist one signature line that returns at the climax and at the end.
- Villains are human scale and believable. Where it fits, include a real-life lesson the viewer can use.

## Facts
At least five real, checkable facts. State only what you are sure of. If unsure, write around it. The power is fiction; physics and biology around it are real, and that is where the tension comes from. For medical subjects add "not medical advice" to the description.

## Sequels
Keep every name, rule and number. Open by jumping ahead, recap in one or two sentences, raise the stakes personally, reuse a line or detail from the previous episode.

## Final checklist
Length, one paragraph, short sentences, ellipsis count, numbers as words, one rule that decides the climax, one two-way choice where people win, one planted detail paid off, five real facts with nothing invented, the call to action, natural Vietnamese that does not read like a translation.
